The outgoing keeper left three things behind: a set of brass keys, a logbook with forty years of weather in it, and a note that said only, "Don't trust the radio after midnight."

Meret read the note twice, then folded it into the logbook where it would stay dry.

The lighthouse itself she'd seen a hundred times from the mainland, a white pencil-stroke against the grey. Up close it was smaller than she'd imagined, and older, the paint gone to lace in patches where the salt had had its way. She climbed the spiral stair with her single suitcase and did not look down until she reached the lamp room, where the whole coast opened out beneath her like a held-open book.

No ships tonight. There were rarely ships anymore. But the light still had to turn.
