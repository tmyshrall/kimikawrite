The well on Marrow Street went dry on a Tuesday, and by Thursday half the town had convinced themselves it was a sign.

Ada Voss didn't believe in signs. She believed in rope, and windlasses, and the particular smell of wet limestone that told you how far down the water table had fallen. She'd smelled it that morning, faint as a memory, coming up out of the Marrow Street well like a held breath finally let go.

"It's not empty," she told the gathering crowd. "It's hiding."

Nobody much liked that answer. But nobody in Thane had a better one, and so, as they always did when the wells went strange, they sent for the diggers.
